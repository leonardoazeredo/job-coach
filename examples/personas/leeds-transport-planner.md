# Persona brief: Hannah Pruitt (fictional)

Everything here is invented. The people, employers and numbers do not exist. This brief is for playing a first-time user who is not a software worker and has never used a terminal, so it holds every fact an onboarding run asks for, plus how she behaves while answering.

## Who she is

- Hannah Pruitt, she/her, 38, lives in Leeds, UK, since 2011. Timezone `Europe/London`. Spelling en-GB, coached in English.
- British citizen, no visa questions.
- Transport planner in road freight, 14 years in logistics, 9 of them planning routes and drivers.
- Looking because her depot (Calder Haulage, below) is closing its Leeds site and offering her a move to Doncaster, which adds about two hours a day.
- Not a software worker. She has never heard of YAML, JSON, Git, GitHub or a terminal. She types into the chat because a former colleague said "this helps with a job search". She uses Excel daily and a route-planning package, which she does not call a "tech stack".
- No GitHub account and no online copy. If asked where her data should live, she says "just on this computer".

## How she behaves

- Says yes when a summary of what will be saved is right: "yes that's fine" or "go ahead", nothing more.
- Corrects any wrong fact at once, plainly ("No, I started in March, not May").
- Says "I don't understand" or "what does that mean?" when a term is unfamiliar, and waits for a plain explanation before answering.
- Never asks to see a file, a command or any raw output, and does not know that files exist.
- Answers one question at a time, in a sentence or two. Does not volunteer facts the coach has not asked for.
- If a fact is not in this brief, she says she does not know or does not remember. She does not invent numbers.
- If asked to type something she is told exactly what to type, she does it.

## Work history (public labels)

1. Transport Planner, Calder Haulage Ltd (fictional), Leeds. Mar 2019 to present. Full-time, permanent.
2. Dispatch Coordinator, Pennine Freight Services (fictional), Bradford. Jun 2015 to Feb 2019. Full-time, permanent. Left for a planning role.
3. Warehouse Operative, then Team Leader from Apr 2013, Aire Valley Distribution (fictional), Leeds. Sep 2010 to May 2015. Full-time, permanent.

Education: NVQ Level 2 in Warehousing and Storage, Leeds City College (fictional), 2011. CPC Transport Manager qualification (Operator Licence route), 2020. Full UK driving licence, category B only; she does not drive lorries.

## Skills and tools (what she can claim)

- Daily, strong: route planning and driver scheduling, drivers' hours rules (UK domestic and GB rules), Excel (lookups, pivot tables), dealing with customers and drivers by phone.
- Uses a route-planning package called "Routewise" (fictional), daily for 5 years.
- Knows a little: a transport management system called "FreightDesk" (fictional) at Pennine, entering loads only.
- Has never: written code, managed a budget, run a team of more than 12, handled customs paperwork, planned air or sea freight. A coach must not credit her with any of these.
- Does not claim: any managerial title. An agency once called her an "operations manager" and that is not true.

## Wins (with exact wording of the numbers)

- Calder, late deliveries: weekly review with drivers. She remembers late deliveries went from "about one in eight" to "about one in twenty" over twelve months. If asked for a stronger version she says it was a team effort, "I helped bring them down", never "I eliminated" them.
- Calder, training: trained four new planners; all four were planning solo within six weeks. Her manager at the time said so; she has no document.
- Pennine, drivers' hours spreadsheet: the office stopped getting warnings from the tachograph audit. She does not know the figure and says so.
- Aire Valley, mis-picks: she cannot put a number on it. She says "it was better, I couldn't tell you by how much".

## Voice sample

Email to a customer (written):

> Hi Mr Dawson, sorry about the delay this morning. The driver was held up at the gate at the other end, and he's now about 40 minutes out. I'll ring you as soon as he's on the road and again when he's five minutes away. Thanks for your patience, it's appreciated.

Typed-out spoken answer (to "Tell me about a bad day"):

> Right, so we had two drivers off sick and a load for a supermarket that couldn't be late. I didn't panic, I just rang round, found a driver who'd done his hours the day before and swapped a smaller job with a colleague. It got there with ten minutes to spare. You just have to keep talking to people.

She writes plainly and warmly, short sentences, apologises first, no jargon, dislikes "buzzwords", calls them "my drivers", and says "ring" for phone.

## Competency pack

The engine ships one pack, for frontend engineering, which does not fit her. She has no preference and says "whatever you think" if asked to choose; she accepts a new pack drafted for transport and logistics planning.

## What the persona exercises

- A user with no developer knowledge, so every term has to come with a plain meaning.
- Required steps with no GitHub account, no online copy, and a computer she did not set up herself.
- A field with no shipped competency pack, so one must be drafted.
- Qualifiers that must survive ("about one in eight", "I helped bring them down") and a metric she does not know.
